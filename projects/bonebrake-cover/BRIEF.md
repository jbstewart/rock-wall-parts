> ## Artwork cut density — cleared 2026-10-15, with one judgement call open
>
> Brian flagged plasma cuts "within 1/8" on the face. Two gating checks now measure it. **Both pass on `ArtLayer-Final-Rev-05.svg`, with the art at FULL FIT (scale 0.0199 — no clearance backoff).** The panel is cuttable; what follows is why, and the one number still resting on judgement.
>
> **The first version of these checks was wrong and is worth recording.** It compared pairs of points on the cut contours and reported the smallest gap. That is meaningless for a *tapered point* of steel: its width goes to zero at the tip by definition, so the "minimum" it reports is an artefact of how far back from the tip the sampling filter happens to land. It reported the artwork edit of 2026-10-15 as a 24% regression (0.0735 → 0.0602) when the edit was in fact a **7.5% improvement**. Brian disbelieved the result, which is the only reason it was caught.
>
> **What replaced it:** rasterise at 0.005, erode by floor/2, and look at what the erosion removed. Stable under reshaping, and it separates the two things the old metric conflated — a tapered tip that thins out (cosmetic; plasma cannot hold a knife edge) from a long parallel-sided ribbon (the scrap risk, because a ribbon has no adjacent metal to sink the arc heat). So the gate is on the **length of a too-thin run**, not on a minimum width.
>
> | | floor | longest run | gate |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | steel between cuts | 0.1196 (1× t, 11ga actual) | **0.252** | 0.957 |
> | cut-away art | 0.110 (2× kerf) | **0.133** | 0.957 |
>
> 0.32 in² of steel is thinner than the floor (0.11% of the panel) in 20 ribbons, and 0.17 in² of art (0.24%) in 12. All of it is short tapers; nothing approaches a ribbon.
>
> - **`MAX_THIN_RUN = 8 × thickness = 0.957" is the judgement call.** The floors are process limits; this one is reasoning — below roughly that length, surrounding metal sinks the heat. The current worst run clears it 3.8×, so there is real margin, but the number has not been proven on steel. A 4 × 4 coupon of the densest band (y −12.0 … −9.5, which holds 11 of the 20 ribbons) settles it before a 41" blank is committed.
> - **Cut ordering in the dense band** is the one process lever available on this job: run adjacent contours non-consecutively so each thin feature cools before its neighbour is cut. Split the profile op or reorder it rather than letting Fusion choose.
> - **The `revC` files in `output/` are stale** — generated from the superseded artwork, uncommitted, never released. An artwork change is a form/fit/function change, so the next generation must **bump `REV` to D first, then run**. Running before the bump overwrites outputs under the wrong rev letter; that has already happened once on this job.
> - Locations are reported as **(u, v) normalised to the artwork bounding box, origin lower left** — an SVG carries no dimensions, so (u, v) is the only coordinate that survives the round trip into a drawing program.
> - `webmap.py` is the iteration loop: edit the SVG, run it, see what moved. It imports the same `thin_features()` the gate uses, so the map cannot disagree with the checks.

## Datums / stackup (brick → out)

Origin = center of frame back face; +Z out of brick. Frame 0.25×BAR flat bar, mitered + welded, outer corners radiused → z [0, 0.25]. Middle 11ga (nominal 1/8) gray → [0.25, 0.375]. Face 11ga black, art cut through → [0.375, 0.50].

## Base (Rev C — new)

Two pieces of 1/4 × 1-1/2 flat bar, **8.5" long**, laid flat one on the other and **plug-welded** through the LOWER bar (3× 9/16" holes on its centreline at x 0, ±3.5 — 9/16 is the AWS D1.1 plug minimum for 1/4 plate, `t + 5/16`). Welds ground flush to the BOTTOM face so the base doesn't rock on concrete; the UPPER bar's top face is left clean because **both the frame and the panel unit seat on it**. That is why the holes go in the bottom bar, not the top.

Finished base 8.5 L × 1.5 deep × 0.5 tall, occupying y [−H/2−0.5, −H/2], z [0, 1.5] — back edge flush with the frame's back face, so 1.0" projects forward of the finished cover face. Top front edge and both top ends get a ~1/8" round-over (Brian promised a toe break; the base finishes 0.75" below the 1.25" carpet anyway, so this is belt-and-braces).

**Welded to the frame** — the frame's bottom bar stands on the base, fillet-welded along the full 8.5" front seam. Welding rather than leaving it loose is what guarantees the base top and the frame bottom are coplanar: the panel unit's bottom edge lands on that surface, and a loose base sitting a hair proud or low would show a step or a gap at the floor line. The cost is install (see below).

## Finish (DECIDED)

**Michelle decided 2026-10-16: Black / Burnished Amber / Black rivets.** Face + frame: hammered black (forge texture, hides mill scale; powder coat $100 = too rich). Middle: **burnished amber** field — confirm the exact product before spraying, and if it is textured the texture scale must stay fine (it shows only through the cutout slivers). Middle EDGES black. **Rivets black** — the catalog rivet (`97484A245`) is bright 1100 aluminum, so this needs either a black rivet sourced or the 6 heads blackened after bucking; settle it before paint day, because bucking a set rivet mars the head and a touch-in has to match hammered black on a show face. Paint after drill/tap, before riveting; mask tapped holes.

**Two ops, one gcode file per panel.** Operations are not programs: post the SETUP and Fusion writes both ops into a single NC file in browser-tree order, same torch throughout, so Jimmie runs one file. **Pilot op goes first** — pierce the holes while the blank is still fully attached and rigid. Brian hit the other half of this on the coupon 2026-10-16: with default linking the pilots are silently DISCARDED, because a 0.059 pierce clearance plus a 0.020 lead needs 0.079" inside a 0.0625" radius. That is what the small-hole recipe exists to prevent, and it is now spelled out on the coupon sheet too rather than only cross-referenced to the CAM sheet.

## Plasma / CAM

Panel DXF holes are **1/8" PILOTS** (drilled to final per schedules; clamp face+middle, drill pairs together). Pilots cut via the **small-hole CAM recipe** (settings finalized 2026-08-25): separate holes-only 2D Profile op, Passes→Sideways Comp = CENTER, Linking tab: Leads→Lead-In (Entry) + Lead-Out (Exit) both UNCHECKED, Piercing→Pierce Clearance = 0 (normal comp refuses < ~3/16; default 0.059 pierce clearance can't fit). Art + outline = normal op. Kerf stays in CAM. Full per-op setup is the generated **CAM sheet** (`…-camsheet-revX.md`) — set up Fusion from it, not from memory.

**The two cut-density checks (added 2026-10-15)** are the only ones here that a parameter change cannot satisfy — they measure the *artwork*, which is an imported file. `MIN_WEB` (1× thickness) and `MIN_SLIVER` (2× kerf) are process limits of the ArcDroid on 11ga and are not relaxation candidates. `MAX_THIN_RUN` is engineering judgement (8× thickness) and is the number a coupon should confirm; raise it only with a coupon result behind it, never to get a build out. Measurement is by rasterised erosion, not contour-pair sampling — see the note at `RASTER_PITCH` for the taper problem that forced the rewrite. Adds ~10 s to a run.

**The self-intersection check (added 2026-10-15)** exists because Rev D art passed every connectivity test and still would not cut. Fusion refused to extrude one tree; the DXF re-imported as 55 of 55 CLOSED wires, worst endpoint gap 2.4e-9", zero T-junctions, every node exactly degree 2. The defect was invisible to all of it: three contours crossed in their **interiors**, where an SVG cusp doubles back a few thousandths past its own node. A closed-but-self-intersecting loop cannot bound a face, so Fusion will neither extrude it nor accept it as a 2D profile. Worst of the three was 0.0078" out — 0.15× kerf, i.e. physically undetectable in cut steel but fatal to CAM. `despike_art()` trims both curves back to the crossing when the spike is under `MAX_SPIKE` (one kerf, since a self-overlap narrower than the kerf cannot survive the cut); anything larger would change the part and FAILS instead, naming the coordinates so the artwork gets fixed. Measured cost of the repair on Rev-04 art: **+1.4e-5 in² on 69.3 in² (+0.00002%), bounding box unchanged**, and the worst endpoint gap in the shipped DXF goes from 2.4e-9" to 7.0e-8" — still three orders of magnitude under any CAD import tolerance. Detection is an exact curve/curve intersection behind a bounding-box prefilter; the backoff loop reuses the first pass's edge pairs (which edges cross is scale-invariant) and the accepted scale is then re-searched from scratch, so the gate never trusts a reused result. Adds ~25 s to a run.

**The repair's own second bug, worth recording.** Trimming both curves to their crossing keeps whatever lay BEYOND it, and where a cusp had eaten most of a short curve that remainder is a stub: Rev D shipped a **0.0016"** and a **0.0030"** LINE into a file whose next-shortest edge was 0.0588", both inside the coupon's Zone A. Fusion merges endpoints that close on import, which collapses a stub, opens the loop and loses the profile — the original symptom, moved one step down the pipe, and it is what Brian saw when the coupon still would not extrude after the self-intersection was fixed. `_bridge_spike()` now handles that case: it keeps the curve with the longer surviving piece, pulls it back from the shared node until the straight run to the other curve's far end exceeds **`MIN_FRAGMENT` = 0.010"**, and replaces the short curve with that bridge, so nothing sub-tolerance survives. 0.010 is 5× under the kerf (invisible in cut steel) and 10× above the thousandth-of-an-inch band where importers merge points; the two bridges came out 0.0130" and 0.0167". A check proves it, and if no pull-back satisfies both floors the crossing is reported UNREPAIRED and the build fails rather than shipping a stub. **Measured cost of the whole repair on Rev-04 art: +1.4e-5 in² on 69.3 in² (+0.00002%), bounding box unchanged.**

**What actually stopped Fusion extruding the right-hand tree — one curve out of 926.** Settled 2026-10-16 by experiment after three wrong theories. The file passed every structural test: 55 of 55 loops closed, zero interior crossings, no two loops within 0.04" of each other, shortest entity 0.013", worst endpoint gap 2.4e-9", every node exactly degree 2, all Z flat, all splines clamped and non-rational — and the loop that FAILED was the better-behaved of the two (99 entities, perimeter 22.3") while the left-hand tree that worked was more convoluted (166 entities, 39.1") and had the *tighter* self-clearance (0.0063" against 0.0137"). No geometric story fits that, so the next step was isolation, not another theory: re-emit the file with every loop as a closed polyline, and re-emit each suspect loop alone in its own file, as splines and as a polyline. Result — whole coupon flattened: profiles. Left tree alone, splines: profiles. Left tree alone, polyline: profiles. Right tree alone, polyline: profiles. **Right tree alone, splines: refuses.** One variable left: the spline records of that loop. A scan for degeneracies found exactly one curve in the whole job whose last two control points are IDENTICAL, so its derivative vanishes at that end — the signature of an SVG corner node whose handle sits exactly on its anchor, which Affinity emits routinely. Fusion will not build a profile from a region containing one, and flattening erases it, which is why every polyline test passed. The same curve sat in the panel face DXF as well, so **the panel's right tree would have failed identically** — it simply had not been tested.

**The fix, and why in-memory rather than at export.** `degenerate_art_curves()` finds them by reading control-point poles off the OCC curve, and `_undegenerate_wire()` refits each one as a plain spline interpolating the SAME curve at 0.002" (27x under the kerf), one edge in and one edge out so the backoff pass's cached edge indices stay valid. Doing it on the art rather than on the written DXF means the STEP assembly is fixed too, since it carries the same curve. Measured: art area and bounding box unchanged to six decimals (69.333533 in², 8.000000 x 30.265900), exported loop areas unchanged to five (right tree -6.42858 against -6.42854). A 40th check gates it, and `repair_degenerate_splines()` stays on the DXF writer as a belt-and-braces net that re-reads what it wrote and raises rather than ship an unprofileable program — it now finds nothing to do. **The lesson is the method, not the defect: when every property you can measure says a file is clean and it still fails, stop measuring and start isolating variables.** `dxfprobe.py` exists to do that: it writes polyline and single-loop variants of any DXF on demand.

**`artfit.py` — the file artwork edits are measured against.** The released panel DXF carries the art at whatever scale the clearance backoff settled on, which is smaller than full fit. Editing the SVG against that file and reasoning in its coordinates is how Rev-03 pushed a branch *into* a bolt. `artfit.py` writes `…-artfit-DIAGNOSTIC.dxf` (gitignored, **not a cut program**): the panel outline, the art at **full fit, despiked**, and three rings per fastener hole — `HOLE` as-built drilled size, `KEEPOUT` hole + 0.05 (what the check gates on), `WEBTARGET` hole + half kerf + `MIN_WEB` (clear this and the web is sound) — plus a `VIOLATION` cross on every art point inside its WEBTARGET ring. The companion `.md` tables every hole worst-first and, for each violation, the move required in **SVG user units with direction**, noting that `flip_y=True` makes a positive dy move the feature down on the finished panel. Run it before touching artwork and after, never the released panel.

**A self-intersecting face also made the kernel lie.** With the defect present, `art_hole_overlap()` reported **0.000000** at bolt (−3.500, −6.604) while the art truly invaded that keep-out by 0.000772 in² — OCC booleans against an invalid face fail globally, not just near the defect, so the auto-backoff never fired and Rev C/D art shipped at full size on a false clearance. Despiking therefore happens **before** the clearance test, not after. See open item 2 for the consequence.

**Consciously relaxed at Rev C:** "side bars bear on brick past the jag" demanded a further 0.25" of bar beyond the widest jag. That margin hedged an *estimated* opening; the opening is now measured at 5.5 max and the cover width is no longer free (it is set by the bare strip). The check became "fully on brick when centred" (2.750 ≥ 2.750, exactly), and the hedge was re-expressed as the physical thing it stood in for: **1.25" of the 1.5" bar still on brick with the cover 0.25" off centre**. Not deleted — replaced with something truer.
